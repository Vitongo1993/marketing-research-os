# Selection Logic

The books listed here were selected because they support at least one of the following areas:

```text id="wgwwlx"
1. Customer Journey
2. Customer Experience
3. CRM and Relationship Marketing
4. Lead Management and Sales Funnel
5. Marketing Automation and Artificial Intelligence
```

The goal is not to create a large bibliography.

# 1. Marketing Management

## Kotler, Keller and Chernev — Marketing Management

## Main Area

Marketing Management, customer value, marketing strategy and customer-centered business logic.

## Relevance to the TCC

This book can support the general marketing foundation of the study.

It helps contextualize the importance of creating, communicating and delivering value to customers.

## Possible Use in the TCC

Use this book to support:

* marketing management
* customer value
* customer orientation
* segmentation and targeting
* digital marketing context
* customer relationship logic
* sales funnel and marketing performance

## Connection to Telex

The Telex case shows that marketing performance depends not only on generating demand, but also on delivering a customer journey capable of transforming interest into store attendance and sales.

## Recommended Priority

Essential.

# 6. Services Marketing

## Zeithaml, Bitner and Gremler — Services Marketing

## Main Area

Services marketing, service quality, customer expectations and service delivery.

## Relevance to the TCC

This is one of the most important books for the Telex case because hearing care is a service-intensive business.

It helps explain how customers evaluate service quality and how service processes affect perception.

## Possible Use in the TCC

Use this book to support:

* services marketing
* service quality
* customer expectations
* service delivery
* service encounter
* customer participation
* perceived risk
* responsiveness

## Connection to Telex

The customer journey involves trust, physical attendance, expert evaluation and follow-up.

Delays, unclear responsibility and multiple handoffs may affect perceived service quality.

## Recommended Priority

Essential.
