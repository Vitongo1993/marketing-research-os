# Purpose

This document defines the **Customer Journey Framework (CJF)** within the Marketing Research OS.

It is a hybrid model that combines:

- academic theory (marketing + consumer behavior)
- practical CRM and sales funnel applications
- AI-assisted journey optimization
- real-world business implementation (Telex Soluções Auditivas)

The objective is to provide a **structured, reusable and diagnostic framework** for analyzing, designing and optimizing customer journeys in both B2C and B2B contexts.

---

# Concept Definition

The Customer Journey is the complete sequence of interactions between a customer and a brand, from the first contact to post-purchase experience.

In MROS, the journey is not only descriptive.

It is treated as a:

> **diagnostic system for identifying friction, opportunities and conversion inefficiencies.**

---

# Core Principle

A customer journey is not linear in reality.

However, for analytical purposes, it is structured into stages to enable:

- measurement
- optimization
- experimentation
- automation (AI + CRM)

---

# Customer Journey Structure (MROS Model)

```text
Awareness
    ↓
Consideration
    ↓
Intent
    ↓
Conversion
    ↓
Onboarding
    ↓
Retention
    ↓
Advocacy
```

Each stage contains:

- customer behavior
- business objective
- friction points
- KPIs
- optimization opportunities

---

# Stage 1 — Awareness

## Objective
Generate attention and attract potential customers.

## Channels
- Paid Ads (Google, Meta)
- Organic content
- Referrals
- Offline awareness (store presence)

## Key Metrics
- impressions
- CTR
- reach
- cost per lead (CPL)

## Common Frictions
- unclear messaging
- low relevance targeting
- weak creative alignment

---

# Stage 2 — Consideration

## Objective
Increase interest and qualification of the lead.

## Behaviors
- website visits
- WhatsApp interaction
- form submission
- content engagement

## Frictions
- long forms
- slow response time
- lack of personalization

---

# Stage 3 — Intent

## Objective
Transform interest into purchase intent.

## Signals
- asking for pricing
- requesting appointment
- comparing options

## Frictions
- uncertainty
- lack of trust
- complex decision journey

---

# Stage 4 — Conversion

## Objective
Close the sale or appointment.

## Key Drivers
- trust in brand
- clarity of offer
- human assistance (CSO / sales rep)

## Frictions
- delayed contact
- misalignment between lead and store
- lack of scheduling integration (critical issue in Telex case)

---

# Stage 5 — Onboarding

## Objective
Ensure successful first experience.

## In Telex Context
- check-up auditivo
- product testing
- first usage guidance

## Frictions
- poor explanation of product use
- lack of follow-up
- emotional hesitation

---

# Stage 6 — Retention

## Objective
Maintain customer engagement and satisfaction.

## Strategies
- follow-up calls
- usage monitoring
- support assistance
- CRM nurturing

## Metrics
- retention rate
- follow-up response rate
- return visits

---

# Stage 7 — Advocacy

## Objective
Transform customers into promoters.

## Indicators
- referrals
- reviews
- testimonials
- word-of-mouth

## Frictions
- lack of incentive to refer
- weak emotional connection
- absence of structured advocacy program

# CRM Integration Model

The customer journey must be fully integrated with CRM systems.

Core principles:

- every lead must have a journey state
- transitions must be tracked automatically
- friction points must be logged
- conversion bottlenecks must be visible

---

# Telex Soluções Auditivas — Applied Case

This framework is directly applied to the Telex case study.

## Key Problem Identified

- fragmented lead routing
- centralized call center bottleneck
- lack of store-level visibility
- delayed response times
- loss of leads during transition

## Journey Breakdown Issue

The main failure occurred between:

> Intent → Conversion

Due to:

- inefficient scheduling system
- lack of direct store access
- incentive misalignment in call center

---

## Implemented Solution (MROS-Aligned)

- chatbot-based initial qualification
- simplified lead capture (Name + City + Store)
- direct routing to nearest store
- CSO (store-level sales ownership)
- reduced dependency on centralized call center

---

## Observed Impact

- +100% increase in lead entry volume
- improved scheduling efficiency
- higher conversion rates
- reduced lead loss during transition

# Diagnostic Framework (Core Tool)

The CJF is also a diagnostic tool.

For any journey, ask:

## 1. Where is the friction?

## 2. Where are leads dropping?

## 3. Is there delay in response?

## 4. Is ownership clear at each stage?

## 5. Is CRM integrated with journey flow?

## 6. Is AI improving or complicating the process?

---

# Optimization Principles

Customer journeys should always be:

- frictionless
- measurable
- automated where possible
- human-driven where necessary
- continuously optimized

---

# Common Journey Failures

- overcomplicated forms
- centralized bottlenecks
- lack of ownership per stage
- no CRM visibility
- delayed follow-ups
- disconnected channels
