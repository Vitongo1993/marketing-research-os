# Core Concept of Scientific Research in Marketing

Scientific research in marketing is defined as a systematic process of:

* identifying phenomena in markets and consumer behavior
* formulating testable questions
* collecting empirical or secondary data
* analyzing evidence using structured methods
* generating interpretable and reproducible knowledge

The objective is not only to describe reality, but to explain, predict and improve marketing-related decisions.

---

# Types of Research

## 1. Exploratory Research

Used when the problem is not clearly defined.

Characteristics:

* flexible design
* qualitative focus
* hypothesis generation
* literature-driven insights

---

## 2. Descriptive Research

Used to describe characteristics of a population or phenomenon.

Characteristics:

* structured data collection
* surveys and observational methods
* statistical summaries
* pattern identification

---

## 3. Causal (Explanatory) Research

Used to identify cause-effect relationships.

Characteristics:

* controlled variables
* experimental or quasi-experimental design
* hypothesis testing
* statistical inference

---

# Research Approaches

## Qualitative Approach

Focus:

Methods:

* interviews
* focus groups

* ethnography

---

## Quantitative Approach

Focus:

* measurement
* statistical validation
* generalization

Methods:

* surveys
* experiments
* structured datasets
* econometric models

---

## Mixed Methods

Combination of qualitative and quantitative approaches.

Used when:

* deeper context is needed
* validation across methods is required
* complex phenomena are studied

---

# Scientific Validity

## Internal Validity

Ensures that observed effects are truly caused by the studied variables.

## External Validity

Ensures that results can be generalized to other contexts.

## Construct Validity

Ensures that the correct concepts are being measured.

## Reliability

Ensures consistency and reproducibility of results.

---

# Hypothesis Development

A hypothesis is a testable statement derived from theory or observation.

## Structure

* H1: Expected relationship
* H0: Null hypothesis (no relationship)

## Example

* H1: Reducing friction in the lead capture process increases conversion rates
* H0: There is no relationship between lead capture friction and conversion rates

# Literature Review Principles

The literature review must:

* identify key academic authors
* map theoretical evolution
* compare competing models
* identify gaps in knowledge
* support hypothesis formation

---

# Evidence Hierarchy

The project adopts the following hierarchy of evidence:

1. Meta-analyses
2. Systematic reviews
3. Peer-reviewed journal articles
4. Academic books
5. Official institutional reports
6. Industry publications
7. Secondary sources (supporting only)

# Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence may assist in:

* summarizing literature
* organizing concepts
* identifying patterns
* generating research questions
* supporting hypothesis formulation

However:

* AI cannot replace scientific judgment
* AI outputs must be validated
* hallucinated references are strictly prohibited

---

# Common Research Errors

Researchers must avoid:

* unclear research questions
* lack of theoretical grounding
* sampling bias
* confirmation bias
* over-reliance on AI-generated outputs
* weak methodological alignment
