# Purpose

This document defines the **CRM Framework (CRMF)** within the Marketing Research OS.

The framework establishes how customer relationships are structured, tracked, managed and optimized across the entire customer journey.

It connects:

- Customer Journey design
- Lead management operations
- Sales execution
- Post-sales relationship building
- AI-assisted automation

The CRM Framework transforms customer journey theory into an operational system.

---

# Core Concept

A CRM system is not just a database of customers.

In the MROS model, CRM is defined as:

> **A dynamic system for managing customer interactions across all lifecycle stages, enabling data-driven decisions, automation and personalized engagement.**

---

# CRM Structural Model

The CRM system is composed of five interconnected layers:

```text
1. Data Capture Layer
        ↓
2. Lead Management Layer
        ↓
3. Interaction Layer
        ↓
4. Conversion Layer
        ↓
5. Retention Layer
```

Each layer has its own function, metrics and optimization logic.

---

# Layer 1 — Data Capture

## Objective
Collect structured and actionable customer data.

## Sources
- Paid Ads (Meta, Google)
- Landing pages
- WhatsApp entry points
- Organic traffic
- Referrals

## Key Inputs
- Name
- Location
- Contact information
- Intent signals

## Critical Principle
> Data must be minimal, relevant and structured.

Overcollection reduces conversion efficiency.

---

# Layer 2 — Lead Management

## Objective
Organize and qualify incoming leads.

## Processes
- Lead segmentation
- Qualification (cold, warm, hot)
- Routing to appropriate channel
- Assignment to responsible agent or store

## Key Metrics
- Lead response time
- Qualification rate
- Drop-off rate

## Telex Insight
A major failure occurs when lead ownership is unclear between centralized call center and physical stores.

---

# Layer 3 — Interaction Layer

## Objective
Manage communication between company and customer.

## Channels
- WhatsApp
- Phone
- Email
- Chatbots
- In-person (store)

## Key Principle
> Speed and context determine conversion probability.

## Frictions
- delayed response
- fragmented communication
- lack of personalization
- excessive handoffs between agents

---

# Layer 4 — Conversion Layer

## Objective
Transform leads into customers.

## Conversion Types
- Appointment booking
- Product testing
- Purchase decision
- Contract closure

## Key Drivers
- trust
- clarity of offer
- human support
- availability of scheduling

## Critical Insight (Telex Case)
Conversion failure often occurs due to:

- lack of real-time store availability
- centralized scheduling bottlenecks
- misaligned incentives in call center structures

---

# Layer 5 — Retention Layer

## Objective
Maximize customer lifetime value (CLV).

## Activities
- onboarding support
- usage follow-up
- satisfaction tracking
- re-engagement campaigns

## Metrics
- retention rate
- churn rate
- lifetime value (LTV)
- NPS (Net Promoter Score)

# Telex Soluções Auditivas — CRM Diagnosis

## Identified Problems

- centralized call center bottleneck
- lack of store-level visibility
- delayed lead response
- incentive misalignment (call center vs stores)
- no real-time scheduling integration

---

## Structural Breakdown

The critical failure point was:

> Lead Management → Conversion Layer

Because:

- call center acted as unnecessary intermediary
- stores lacked direct control over scheduling
- leads lost momentum during transfer

---

## Implemented CRM Redesign

- chatbot-based entry qualification
- simplified lead capture (Name + City + Store)
- direct routing to nearest store
- CSO ownership at store level
- reduced dependency on centralized call center

---

## Resulting Impact

- increased lead flow (+100%)
- improved response time
- higher appointment conversion
- reduced operational friction

# CRM Optimization Principles

An optimized CRM system must be:

- fast
- decentralized where necessary
- data-driven
- integrated with journey stages
- automated where possible
- human-supervised where critical

---

# Common CRM Failures

- excessive centralization
- lack of ownership clarity
- poor integration with sales teams
- slow response times
- fragmented communication channels
- overcomplex data capture

---

# Diagnostic Checklist

To evaluate any CRM system, ask:

## 1. Who owns the lead at each stage?

## 2. How fast is the first response?

## 3. Is data structured and minimal?

## 4. Are transitions between stages seamless?

## 5. Is CRM integrated with real operations or just reporting?

## 6. Where do leads get lost?
