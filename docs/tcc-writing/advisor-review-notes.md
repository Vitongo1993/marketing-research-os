# 5. Specific Objectives

## Current Specific Objectives

```text id="zvs89h"
1. Map the customer journey before the intervention, identifying the main points of friction between capture, service, scheduling and attendance.
2. Describe the new service flow implemented in 2025, including WhatsApp entry, chatbot, CRM, automation and store ownership.
3. Compare the main commercial funnel indicators before and after the journey redesign.
4. Evaluate changes in response time and time until scheduling after the intervention.
5. Analyze the operational effects of transferring responsibility from the call center to the stores.
6. Discuss the trade-offs resulting from reduced entry friction, especially the increase in ghost or lower-quality leads.
7. Identify managerial and academic implications of customer journey redesign in B2C service companies.
```

```text id="bz26ee"
1. Map the 2024 customer journey and identify operational friction points.

3. Compare funnel indicators before and after the redesign.
4. Discuss the operational and managerial implications of the redesign.
```

# 11. Causal Language

## Current Position

The study avoids strong causality and uses association-based language.

## Advisor Validation Questions

```text id="alpyrc"
[ ] Is the causal language sufficiently cautious?
[ ] Should "impact" be avoided in the title or research question?
[ ] Should "effect" be avoided?
[ ] Is "associated with" preferable throughout the text?
[ ] Are hypotheses acceptable in a case study?
[ ] Should hypotheses be replaced by analytical propositions?
```

## Important Alternative

If the advisor thinks "hypotheses" are too strong for the study design, replace them with:

```text id="xqp8mp"
analytical propositions
```

Example:

```text id="4qjoyp"
P1 — The reduction of entry friction is associated with increased lead volume.
```

Instead of:

```text id="go0t31"
H1 — The reduction of entry friction increases lead volume.
```
