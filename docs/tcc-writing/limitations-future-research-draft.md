# Limitations and Future Research

This chapter presents the main limitations of the study and suggests future research opportunities based on the analysis of the Telex Soluções Auditivas case.

The objective is to clarify the boundaries of the research, avoid unsupported generalizations and identify additional analyses that could strengthen the understanding of customer journey redesign, CRM automation, lead management and commercial funnel performance.

Because this study is based on a single case and a before-and-after comparison, its findings should be interpreted with methodological caution.

---

# 1. General Limitation of the Study

The main limitation of this study is that it analyzes one organizational case in a specific context.

The case focuses on Telex Soluções Auditivas and its customer journey redesign between 2024 and 2025.

Although the case provides relevant evidence about the relationship between journey redesign, CRM automation, store ownership and funnel performance, the findings cannot be automatically generalized to all companies, sectors or service contexts.

The study is useful for analytical interpretation, but it does not provide statistical generalization.

Its value lies in understanding a real organizational transformation in depth.

---

# 2. Absence of Control Group

The study does not include a control group.

The analysis compares the performance of the commercial funnel before and after the redesign, but it does not compare the redesigned model with a similar group that did not receive the intervention.

This means that the observed improvements cannot be attributed exclusively to the journey redesign.

The increase in leads, appointments, attendance and sales may be associated with the new model, but other variables may also have influenced the results.

Therefore, the study should not state that the redesign caused the improvement.

The correct interpretation is:

```text id="so95ov"
The results suggest an association between the redesigned journey and improved funnel performance.
```

Not:

```text id="j66xba"
The redesigned journey caused the improvement in funnel performance.
```

---

# 3. Before-and-After Comparison Limitation

The study uses a before-and-after comparison between 2024 and 2025.

This design is useful for observing changes over time, but it has limitations.

A before-and-after comparison does not isolate the effect of the intervention from other changes that may have occurred during the same period.

For example, the following factors may have changed between 2024 and 2025:

* media investment;
* campaign strategy;
* promotional offers;
* commercial conditions;
* price policy;
* sales team training;
* store performance;
* product availability;
* seasonality;
* customer profile;
* regional demand;
* market competition;
* internal management routines.

Because these variables were not controlled, the results must be interpreted carefully.

---

# 4. Possible Influence of Media Investment

One important limitation is the lack of complete media investment data in the current analysis.

The study compares funnel indicators, but it does not fully evaluate whether the company increased or changed its investment in paid media between 2024 and 2025.

If media investment increased significantly, part of the lead growth may be explained by increased acquisition effort, not only by reduced friction in the journey.

Future versions of the analysis should include:

* total media investment by period;
* media investment by channel;
* cost per lead;
* campaign volume;
* campaign objective;
* channel mix;
* creative strategy;
* audience targeting;
* seasonality of campaigns.

Without this information, the study cannot separate the effect of journey redesign from the effect of media investment.

---

# 5. Incomplete CAC Analysis

The cost analysis presented in the study is limited to estimated operational cost.

It does not represent full Customer Acquisition Cost.

The current cost analysis considers the operational layer related to:

* call center structure;
* operators;
* CRM;
* chatbot;
* automation tools;
* selected process costs.

However, it does not include:

* paid media investment;
* creative production;
* agency cost;
* store-level operational costs;
* taxes;
* employment charges;
* benefits;
* commissions outside the mapped structure;
* management overhead;
* rent;
* utilities;
* equipment;
* indirect administrative costs.

Therefore, the correct term is:

```text id="eht44l"
estimated operational cost
```

The study should avoid using:

```text id="bdtf54"
CAC
```

unless all acquisition and operational costs are included in the final calculation.

This is a critical limitation because the operational cost reduction does not necessarily represent the total cost reduction of customer acquisition.

---

# 6. Lead Quality Measurement Limitation

The study identifies the increase of ghost leads, cold leads and low-quality contacts as a relevant trade-off of the redesigned model.

However, the available data does not yet allow a precise quantitative analysis of lead quality.

The study does not currently include detailed information such as:

* number of ghost leads;
* number of cold leads;
* number of disqualified leads;
* number of duplicated leads;
* number of leads with invalid phone numbers;
* number of leads who abandoned the bot;
* number of leads who did not respond after store contact;
* number of leads reactivated after follow-up;
* reasons for lead loss;
* quality by channel;
* quality by campaign;
* quality by store.

This limits the ability to measure the real effect of friction reduction on lead quality.

The study can state that the increase in low-quality leads was observed operationally, but it should not present this effect as fully measured unless additional data is included.

---

# 7. Store-Level Analysis Limitation

Another limitation is the absence of a detailed store-level analysis.

The redesigned model depends heavily on store execution, since stores and CSOs became responsible for contacting, scheduling and following up with leads.

However, the current analysis does not isolate performance by store.

This means the study does not yet identify:

* which stores responded faster;
* which stores scheduled more leads;
* which stores had higher attendance rates;
* which stores converted more sales;
* which stores lost more leads;
* which stores had higher no-show rates;
* which CSOs performed better;
* whether the model worked equally across regions.

This limitation is important because decentralization increases the relevance of local execution.

A stronger analysis would compare performance by unit and identify whether the new model produced consistent results across the store network.

---

# 8. Human Response Time Limitation

The study indicates that the 2025 model introduced immediate bot response and reduced the time until scheduling.

However, the analysis should distinguish clearly between:

```text id="jk6fnu"
automated response time
```

and:

```text id="oob6l1"
human response time
```

Immediate bot response does not necessarily mean immediate human service.

The current data does not fully detail the average time between:

* lead entry;
* bot interaction;
* CRM registration;
* store notification;
* first human contact by CSO;
* confirmed appointment.

This limitation matters because customer experience depends not only on receiving an automated message, but also on receiving timely and useful human follow-up.

Future research should measure each step separately.

---

# 9. Customer Perception Limitation

The study focuses mainly on operational and funnel indicators.

It does not include direct customer perception data.

As a result, the analysis cannot confirm how customers perceived the redesigned journey.

The study does not currently answer questions such as:

* Did customers perceive the bot as helpful?
* Did customers feel more confident speaking directly with the store?
* Did customers understand the journey clearly?
* Did automation create frustration for some users?
* Did older customers have difficulty with WhatsApp or bot interaction?
* Did the new model improve perceived trust?
* Did faster scheduling improve satisfaction?

This is an important limitation because customer journey performance should include both operational indicators and customer perception indicators.

---

# 10. Internal Team Perception Limitation

The study also does not include structured qualitative data from internal teams.

It would be useful to collect perceptions from:

* call center operators;
* CSOs;
* store managers;
* marketing team;
* CRM team;
* automation team;
* leadership.

This would help understand implementation challenges, adoption barriers, operational resistance and process improvements from the perspective of those who executed the new model.

Without this qualitative layer, the analysis depends mainly on documented operational data and process interpretation.

---

# 11. Data Source and Extraction Limitation

The study uses internal operational data, but the final version should clarify the exact source and extraction process of each indicator.

Important questions include:

```text id="ml9fsb"
Were the data extracted from CRM?
Were they extracted from Google Sheets?
Were they consolidated manually?
Were duplicated leads removed?
Were canceled appointments counted?
Were no-shows separated from cancellations?
Were sales attributed to the original lead source?
Were the same definitions used in 2024 and 2025?
```

If the data source and extraction rules are not fully described, the reliability of the comparison may be weakened.

The final version of the TCC should include a clear data source table.

---

# 12. Period Equivalence Limitation

The study compares 2024 and 2025 data.

However, the final version must confirm whether the periods are equivalent.

Important questions include:

* Does 2024 represent a full year?
* Does 2025 represent a full year?
* Is 2025 partial, projected or consolidated?
* Were both periods measured using the same calendar months?
* Were campaign periods equivalent?
* Were seasonal effects considered?

If the periods are not equivalent, the comparison must be adjusted or clearly limited.

This is one of the most important validation points before final submission.

---

# 13. Sector-Specific Limitation

The case occurs in the hearing care sector.

This sector has specific characteristics, such as:

* health-related decision-making;
* trust requirement;
* elderly customers;
* family influence;
* need for in-person evaluation;
* product testing;
* adaptation period;
* specialized professional support.

Because of these characteristics, the findings may not apply directly to all B2C sectors.

The case may be more comparable to service businesses that combine digital acquisition with physical or human-assisted appointments, such as:

* healthcare clinics;
* dental clinics;
* aesthetic clinics;
* education services;
* insurance advisory;
* financial advisory;
* automotive sales;
* franchise networks;
* local professional services.

---

# 14. Technology-Specific Limitation

The study analyzes a specific technological configuration involving WhatsApp, bot, RD Conversas, RD CRM, Google Sheets and Pluga.

The findings should not be interpreted as evidence that these specific tools are superior to other tools.

The study evaluates the operational logic of the redesigned flow, not the universal performance of a specific software stack.

The main lesson is about journey architecture, CRM integration and ownership, not about one vendor or platform.

---

# 15. Automation and AI Classification Limitation

The study discusses automation and AI-supported service logic, but it should avoid overstating the technological sophistication of the case.

The Telex model is better described as:

```text id="jwf6n2"
chatbot-supported entry
CRM automation
conversational automation
workflow automation
hybrid human-automation service model
```

It should not be described as:

```text id="y25qx4"
advanced artificial intelligence
autonomous AI decision-making
AI-driven sales system
fully intelligent service automation
```

unless technical evidence supports those claims.

This limitation is important to preserve conceptual accuracy.

---

# 16. Confidentiality Limitation

The case uses internal company information.

Therefore, the final TCC must ensure that sensitive data is not exposed without authorization.

Information that may require anonymization or removal includes:

* customer names;
* phone numbers;
* medical or hearing health data;
* individual customer records;
* employee-level performance;
* store-level confidential results;
* internal compensation structures;
* strategic information;
* private screenshots;
* operational credentials;
* contract values, if considered sensitive.

The final academic version should use aggregated data whenever possible.

---

# 17. Summary of Study Limitations

The main limitations of this study are:

```text id="x27mz9"
1. Single case study.
2. Absence of control group.
3. Before-and-after comparison without causal isolation.
4. Possible influence of media investment.
5. Incomplete CAC calculation.
6. Limited lead quality measurement.
7. Lack of store-level analysis.
8. Limited human response time data.
9. Absence of customer perception data.
10. Absence of structured internal team interviews.
11. Need for clearer data extraction rules.
12. Need to confirm equivalence of 2024 and 2025 periods.
13. Sector-specific context.
14. Technology-specific context.
15. Need to avoid overstating AI sophistication.
16. Confidentiality restrictions.
```

# 18. Future Research Opportunities

The limitations identified open several opportunities for future research.

---

## 18.1 Store-Level Comparative Analysis

Future research should analyze performance by store.

This would allow the company to understand whether the redesigned model works consistently across different units.

Suggested indicators:

* leads received by store;
* first human response time;
* appointment rate;
* attendance rate;
* no-show rate;
* sales conversion rate;
* lost lead rate;
* lead quality by store;
* average time until scheduling;
* CRM update compliance.

This analysis could identify best practices and operational gaps across the network.

---

## 18.2 Lead Quality Study

Future research should investigate the quality of leads generated after the reduction of friction.

Suggested classifications:

```text id="zqe5ex"
Qualified lead
Hot lead
Warm lead
Cold lead
Ghost lead
Lost lead
Disqualified lead
Reactivated lead
```

This would make it possible to evaluate whether the increase in volume is accompanied by enough commercial quality.

---

## 18.3 Full CAC and ROI Analysis

A future study should calculate full Customer Acquisition Cost and return on investment.

This would require inclusion of:

* media investment;
* campaign costs;
* creative costs;
* agency costs;
* CRM and automation costs;
* call center costs;
* store operational costs;
* commissions;
* management costs;
* revenue generated;
* gross margin;
* net contribution.

This would allow the company to evaluate not only operational efficiency, but economic performance.

---

## 18.4 Customer Experience Survey

Future research should collect customer perception data.

Possible survey topics:

* ease of initial contact;
* clarity of WhatsApp flow;
* perception of the bot;
* trust in store contact;
* scheduling satisfaction;
* reasons for no-show;
* reasons for purchase or non-purchase;
* satisfaction with check-up;
* satisfaction with post-sale follow-up.

This would connect operational metrics to perceived customer experience.

---

## 18.5 Qualitative Interviews with Internal Teams

Future research could include interviews with:

* CSOs;
* store managers;
* call center operators;
* marketing team;
* CRM team;
* automation team.

Possible topics:

* implementation challenges;
* resistance to change;
* store-level adaptation;
* CRM adoption;
* lead quality perception;
* bottlenecks after redesign;
* suggestions for improvement.

This would strengthen the qualitative dimension of the case study.

---

## 18.6 No-Show Behavior Analysis

Attendance is a critical step in the Telex funnel.

Future research should analyze no-show behavior in detail.

Suggested variables:

* customer age;
* store location;
* time between lead and appointment;
* confirmation message;
* number of follow-ups;
* source channel;
* day of week;
* appointment time;
* distance to store;
* reason for missing the appointment.

This would help design better appointment confirmation and recovery flows.

---

## 18.7 Bot Abandonment Analysis

Future research should analyze where customers abandon the bot flow.

Suggested indicators:

* start rate;
* completion rate;
* abandonment point;
* repeated question rate;
* human escalation rate;
* unresolved interaction rate;
* city-not-found rate;
* complaint rate;
* time to completion.

This would help improve the conversational journey.

---

## 18.8 Human Response SLA Study

A future study should measure human response time more precisely.

Suggested timestamps:

```text id="yzpmx1"
lead entry time
bot completion time
CRM registration time
store notification time
first CSO contact time
appointment confirmation time
attendance time
sale time
```

This would allow more precise evaluation of where time is gained or lost in the journey.

---

## 18.9 Multi-Case Comparison

Future research could compare the Telex case with other service-based organizations.

Relevant comparison sectors include:

* audiology;
* healthcare clinics;
* dental clinics;
* aesthetics;
* education;
* insurance;
* automotive dealerships;
* franchise networks.

A multi-case design would help evaluate whether the findings are specific to Telex or applicable to other digital-to-offline service journeys.

---

## 18.10 Longitudinal Follow-Up

Future research could analyze whether the results remain stable over time.

A longitudinal analysis could compare:

```text id="v9nszt"
2024
2025
2026
2027
```

This would help identify whether the gains from the redesign were temporary, stable or improving.

It would also help evaluate whether lead quality changes over time as customers and teams adapt to the new model.

---

# 19. Future Managerial Experiments

In addition to academic research, Telex could conduct controlled managerial experiments.

Possible experiments include:

* testing different bot scripts;
* testing shorter versus longer qualification flows;
* testing different response time SLAs;
* testing store-level follow-up sequences;
* testing appointment confirmation messages;
* testing no-show recovery flows;
* testing human-first versus bot-first flows for elderly customers;
* testing prioritization rules for high-intent leads.

These experiments would allow the company to move from observational analysis to more robust evidence about what improves conversion.

---

# 20. Recommended Future Research Questions

Future studies may investigate the following questions:

```text id="fqri9a"
1. How does lead quality vary after reducing entry friction in digital-to-offline service journeys?
2. How does human response time influence attendance rates in appointment-based service businesses?
3. What is the relationship between bot completion rate and appointment conversion?
4. How does store-level ownership affect lead follow-up and sales conversion?
5. Which CRM governance practices reduce lead loss in distributed service networks?
6. How do customers perceive chatbot-supported journeys in health-related services?
7. What factors explain no-show behavior in hearing care appointments?
8. How does automation affect the work routine of CSOs and call center teams?
9. What is the full CAC impact of journey redesign when media investment is included?
10. How sustainable are funnel improvements after customer journey redesign?
```

---

# 21. Final Considerations on Limitations

The limitations of this study do not reduce its relevance.

They define the scope of what can and cannot be concluded.

The study provides evidence that the redesigned Telex journey was associated with improvements in lead volume, scheduling speed, attendance and sales conversion.

However, the findings should be interpreted as part of a real organizational context, not as universal proof.

The strongest contribution of the study is to show how customer journey redesign, CRM automation and store ownership can be analyzed together as part of a broader lead management system.

Future research can strengthen this contribution by adding more granular data, customer perception, store-level comparison, full cost analysis and controlled experiments.
